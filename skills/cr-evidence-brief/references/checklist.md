# Evidence Brief: interpretation checklist

Use this glossary to interpret the supplied records. It is not a vendor specification, a substitute for the customer's policy, or evidence about a real environment.

## Terms and evidence expectations

| Term or control area | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Observation | A supplied record reports a specific state at a specific time. |
| Assertion | A person, policy, questionnaire, or report claims a state; independent verification may be absent. |
| Inference | Reasoning that connects observations; its assumptions remain explicit. |
| Unknown | The available inputs cannot establish the field or claim. |
| Contradiction | Sources disagree after differences in scope and observation time are considered. |

## Synthetic example

The following is an invented training example, not a finding to copy into a report:

An inventory says an agent is installed, while a monitoring export has no recent event for the same asset. The evidence supports an installation claim and a telemetry gap—not a conclusion that the host is unprotected or compromised.

## Reviewer questions

- Every substantive claim resolves to an evidence reference or is labelled as an inference.
- The covered scope and excluded scope are explicit.
- Contradictions and missing information are visible beside their decision impact.
- The handoff distinguishes a recommended next step from a completed action.

## Handoff record

When another analyst continues the work, preserve the assessed scope, observation window, source references, unresolved questions, and the decision owner. Keep this library's files unchanged; working evidence and completed reports belong in the user's approved workspace.
